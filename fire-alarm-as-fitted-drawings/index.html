How to do a fire alarm as-fitted drawing

An as-fitted drawing records what was actually installed. Here is what it needs to show and how to produce one.

General guidance only. Check the standard itself and take advice from your own competent person for the job in front of you. Last reviewed 2 November 2026.

What an as-fitted drawing is

An as-fitted drawing (also called an as-installed or as-built drawing) shows the fire alarm system as it was actually installed, as opposed to the design drawing that says how it was meant to be. It is part of the handover documents under BS 5839-1: drawings of the final installation, including cable run details, that are complete and up to date.

What it should show

  • The panel, repeaters and power supplies, and where they are.
  • Every call point, detector, sounder and visual alarm, in its installed position, with the type and the zone or address.
  • Cable routes, with the cable type and size.
  • Any change from the design drawing, such as moved devices, added devices or a different route.
  • A key to the symbols, and the revision and date.

How to do one, step by step

  1. Start from the design drawing. Use the latest issue, with the floor plans and the zoning.
  2. Mark it up as you install. Note every change on a marked-up copy at the time, on paper or on a tablet. It is much harder to remember afterwards.
  3. Record each device. Position, type, address or zone, and a label that matches what is on the device.
  4. Record the cabling. Show the route of each circuit and the cable type and size. Do this before it is hidden behind ceilings and walls, and take photos where it helps.
  5. Note the changes from design. Make deviations visible on the drawing so the designer, the commissioning engineer and the client can see what moved and why.
  6. Produce the final drawing. Redraw or update the plan, tidy the symbols, add a title block, revision and date, and issue it as a PDF.
  7. Check it against the building. Walk the site with the drawing and confirm that devices and zones match what is on the wall and in the ceiling.
  8. Issue it with the handover. Put it in the O&M manual and give the client a copy. Update it whenever the system is altered.

Common mistakes

  • Issuing the design drawing as if it were as-fitted.
  • Cable routes drawn as straight lines that do not match the real route.
  • Devices renumbered at commissioning but not on the drawing.
  • No revision or date, so no one knows which version is current.

Starting from the design

Begin with the design drawing and mark up everything that changed on site. Installers often keep a working copy during the install and note each device as it is fitted, then clean it up at the end. This is far quicker than trying to recall changes from memory at handover.

What to record for each device

  • Type and reference. Detector type, call point, sounder and so on, with the model if it matters for spares.
  • Address or loop position. The address shown on the panel, and the loop or zone it belongs to.
  • Location. Room name and position on the plan, so anyone can find it.
  • Cable route. Where the cable runs, with the type and size, especially for fire-rated cable.

Deviations from the design

List every deviation on the drawing or a schedule attached to it: moved devices, extra devices, removed devices and any change of cable type. For each one, record the reason and who agreed it. This protects the installer, and it saves the next engineer from having to guess whether a difference is a mistake.

Format and delivery

  • Give each sheet a title block with site name, drawing number, revision and date.
  • Supply both PDF for reading and the editable source file where the contract allows.
  • Put a copy in the O&M manual and tell the client where the master is kept.

Updating after the handover

Any later alteration means an updated drawing with a new revision letter or number. Keep the old versions so the history is visible, but only the current revision should be displayed or handed out.

The as-fitted drawing goes with the zone plan in the O&M manual.

FAQ

Common questions

What is a fire alarm as-fitted drawing?

An as-fitted drawing shows the fire alarm system as it was actually installed, including device positions and cable runs, as opposed to the design drawing. It is part of the handover documents.

Is an as-fitted drawing the same as an as-built drawing?

In practice the terms are used for the same thing: a drawing of the final installation. As-fitted, as-installed and as-built are all common names.

What should a fire alarm as-fitted drawing show?

The panel and power supplies, every device in its installed position with type and zone or address, cable routes with type and size, any change from the design, and a revision and date.

Who produces the as-fitted drawing?

The installer or the commissioning team produces it as the system is installed, from marked-up copies of the design drawings, and issues it at handover. It is updated when the system is altered.

What is the difference between a design drawing and an as-fitted drawing?

A design drawing shows what was intended before the install. An as-fitted drawing shows what was actually fitted, including any changes made on site.

Do as-fitted drawings have to show cable routes?

They should show enough for someone to trace and test the circuit. For fire-rated cable and loop wiring, the route and cable type are normally recorded.

Who should sign off the as-fitted drawing?

The person who carried out the installation checks it against the site, and it is usually issued with the handover documents. Follow the arrangements in your contract and your certification scheme.
