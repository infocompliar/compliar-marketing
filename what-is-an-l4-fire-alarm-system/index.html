What is an L4 fire alarm system?

Category L4 is one of the life-protection categories in BS 5839-1. Here is what it covers, what it does not, and how it differs from L3.

General guidance only. Check the standard itself and take advice from your own competent person for the job in front of you. Last reviewed 2 November 2026.

The short answer

An L4 fire alarm system is a life-protection system with automatic fire detection in the escape routes: the circulation areas and spaces, such as corridors and stairways, that people use to leave the building. The L stands for life protection, and 4 is the level. It is one of the categories described in BS 5839-1.

Rooms that open onto those routes are not covered by default. That is what separates L4 from L3.

L4 compared with L3 and L1

CategoryWhere automatic detection goes
L4Escape routes only: corridors, stairways and similar circulation areas.
L3Escape routes, and the rooms that open onto them.
L2The L3 areas, plus rooms with a high chance of fire or a high risk to life.
L1Every area of the building.

Where L4 is used

L4 suits simpler buildings where the aim is to keep the way out clear of smoke and give people early warning on the route, for example some schools that are used in the evenings. The category is not the installer's choice. It comes from the fire risk assessment and the people responsible for the building, with the insurer's requirements in mind.

What to check when a specification says L4

  • Which areas count as escape routes. Look at the drawing and agree it with the designer, not just the quote.
  • Whether any rooms need detection as well. A specification can combine categories, for example L4 with P2 in a plant room, so each part of the system should trace back to a stated purpose.
  • Any variations from the standard. They should be agreed and written down.
  • The category on the paperwork. It belongs on the design, installation and servicing certificates, so whoever services the system knows what it is meant to do.

If the building changes

If the use of the building changes, or rooms are added off the escape routes, the category may need to change with it. L4 that was right for an empty corridor does not cover a new sleeping area.

What an L4 system is made of

An L4 system has the same building blocks as any addressable or conventional fire alarm: a control panel, detectors, manual call points, sounders or other alarm devices, and a power supply with standby batteries. The category only changes where automatic detectors are fitted. Manual call points and alarm devices are still needed on the same basis as for other categories, so an L4 system is more than a handful of detectors in a corridor.

L4 and the other life-protection categories

L1 to L5 and P1 and P2 are all described in BS 5839-1. L5 is the odd one out: it is a system designed for a specific, stated purpose, for example detection in one particular area to trigger a fire-engineering solution, so it only makes sense with the design reasoning written down. P1 and P2 are property-protection categories, not life protection. M is a manual-only system with call points and alarm devices but no automatic detection.

Limits to be aware of

  • Later warning inside rooms. Without detectors in the rooms, a fire that starts inside a room may only be detected once smoke has reached the escape route, so the category should suit how the building is used.
  • Sleeping risk. Where people sleep, the design question is different and usually points to a higher category. Whoever carries out the fire risk assessment is the right person to decide.
  • Doors and routes. The protection depends on what counts as an escape route in practice. Stored goods or locked doors can change that, which is another reason to review the category when use changes.

Servicing an L4 system

Servicing follows the same pattern as other categories: the system is tested on a regular basis, with a sample of devices tested at each visit so that all are covered across the year, and the results are recorded in the log book. What changes is the list of devices and areas on the schedule, so the category and the as-fitted drawing should be to hand when the engineer arrives.

FAQ

Common questions

What is an L4 fire alarm system?

An L4 system is a life-protection fire alarm with automatic detection in the escape routes, meaning circulation areas such as corridors and stairways. It is one of the categories in BS 5839-1.

What is the difference between L3 and L4?

L3 has automatic detection in escape routes and in the rooms that open onto them. L4 has detection in the escape routes only.

Does an L4 system detect fires inside rooms?

Not by default. Rooms that open onto the escape routes are covered at L3. An L4 specification can add detection in specific rooms for another stated purpose, for example a P2 category in a plant room.

Who decides that a building needs L4?

The category comes from the fire risk assessment and the people responsible for the building, with the insurer's requirements in mind. It is not a decision for the installer or the panel manufacturer.

Is L4 enough for a school?

It depends on the building, how it is used and the fire risk assessment. L4 is sometimes used where the building is mainly occupied in the daytime or where rooms are separated from the escape routes. The responsible person and the designer decide, not the installer alone.

Do manual call points and sounders still apply to L4?

Yes. The category describes where automatic detection is fitted. Manual call points and alarm devices are provided on the same basis as for other categories.

Can an L4 system be upgraded to L3?

Yes, by adding detection in the rooms that open onto the escape routes. The change should be designed, installed and recorded as an alteration, with updated drawings and certificates.
