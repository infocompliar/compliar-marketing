Fire alarm categories explained: L1 to L5, M, P1 and P2

BS 5839-1 describes a fire alarm system by its category. Here is what each letter and number means in practice, with typical buildings, and why the category is not for the installer to pick.

General guidance only. Check the standard itself and take advice from your own competent person for the job in front of you. Last reviewed 22 October 2026.

What a category is

A category is shorthand for how much of a building has automatic detection, and why. It is written into the specification, the design and the certificates, and it tells everyone what the system is meant to achieve. In BS 5839-1 the letters stand for the purpose: M for manual, L for life protection and P for property protection.

The category is decided through the fire risk assessment and by the people responsible for the building, with the insurer's requirements in mind. It is not a decision for the installer or the panel manufacturer, so if a specification does not name one, ask before you price.

The categories at a glance

CategoryIn plain termsTypical use
MManual only. Call points and sounders, with no automatic detectors.Offices, shops, factories and warehouses where the fire risk is low and people are always about. Insurers may ask for a P category as well.
L1Automatic detection in every area of the building.Sleeping risk and the highest life-safety demand, such as hotels, care homes, hostels and hospitals.
L2Detection in the L3 areas plus rooms with a high chance of fire or a high risk to life.Often used with L1 or in premises with sleeping accommodation, such as pubs with rooms.
L3Detection on escape routes and in rooms that open onto them.Buildings with phased evacuation, and a common choice for commercial buildings.
L4Detection only in escape routes such as corridors and stairways.Simpler buildings, for example some schools used in the evenings.
L5Detection designed around a specific fire safety objective, not one of L1 to L4.Inner rooms or non-standard escape arrangements, usually combined with M.
P1Automatic detection throughout, to protect the building and its contents.Where downtime or loss would hurt the business.
P2Automatic detection in defined parts only, to protect property or keep the business running.High-hazard or high-value areas such as kitchens with fryers, plant rooms or server rooms.

Reading a specification

Categories are often combined. A hotel might be L1 in the bedrooms and L2 elsewhere, or a factory might be M with P2 in the plant room. A building can also carry an L category for people and a P category for the insurer. Each part of the system should be traceable to a stated purpose.

When you quote, check three things: which category applies to which area, what the specification says about detector types, and whether any variations from the standard have been agreed and written down.

What changed in 2025

BS 5839-1:2025 made some changes that affect categories in practice. The 2025 edition no longer accepts heat detectors in rooms where people sleep in L2 and L3 systems, and stairway lobbies that used to be treated as low risk are expected to have automatic detection. The detail is in the standard, so read the clause before you design or sign off.

Domestic premises

Categories belong to BS 5839-1, which covers non-domestic premises. Domestic systems are dealt with in BS 5839-6, which uses a different grading, so do not mix the two on a certificate. Houses in multiple occupation sit in between, and the local authority may set its own requirement.

Why the category matters to you

  • It sets the scope of the design, and so the number of devices you are pricing.
  • It tells whoever services the system what they are meant to be testing.
  • It is a line on the certificate. If the category is wrong or missing, the certificate is of little use to anyone.
  • If the building changes use, the category may need to change with it, and the system with it.
FAQ

Common questions

What do the L, M and P mean in fire alarm categories?

M is manual, with call points and sounders only. L is life protection, with automatic detection to protect people. P is property protection, with automatic detection to protect the building and its contents.

What is the difference between L1, L2 and L3?

L1 has automatic detection throughout the building. L2 covers the L3 areas plus rooms with a high fire risk or a high risk to life. L3 covers escape routes and the rooms that open onto them.

Who decides which fire alarm category a building needs?

The category comes from the fire risk assessment and the people responsible for the building, with the insurer's requirements in mind. It is not a decision for the installer or the panel manufacturer.

Can a building have more than one category?

Yes. Categories are often combined, for example L1 in sleeping areas and L2 elsewhere, or M with P2 in a plant room. Each part of the system should be traceable to a stated purpose.
